# highdim_nonlinear_v2 Release Report

## Objective And Scope

This release implements the raw-data protocol in `docs/notes/mathematical explanation/highdim_nonlinear_v2_math.md`. It provides a unified high-dimensional benchmark with three autonomous, deterministic, fully observed systems: L96-40, KS64, and FHN64. The release is intended for downstream forecasting, Koopman, kernel, and representation-learning experiments, but it performs no model training, noise injection, standardization, or algorithm-specific processing.

All trajectories were generated by the new v2 code. No earlier Lorenz96 or PDEID trajectory was imported, transformed, or repackaged.

## Methods And Engineering Choices

L96-40 uses

$$
\dot x_j=(x_{j+1}-x_{j-2})x_{j-1}-x_j+8,
$$

with cyclic indexing, Vern9 production integration, `reltol=1e-10`, `abstol=1e-12`, and `dtmax=0.01`. Each trajectory has burn-in 100 and 2,048 intervals at $\tau_{\mathrm{base}}=0.05$.

KS64 uses the zero-mean periodic equation

$$
\partial_tu=-u\partial_xu-\partial_{xx}u-\partial_{xxxx}u
$$

on $[0,22)$ with 64 Fourier grid points, two-thirds dealiasing, ETDRK4 internal step `0.01`, 64 contour nodes, warm-up 200, and 5,120 recorded intervals. Every ETDRK4 state and tangent step is projected back to the real Hermitian Fourier subspace and zero mode.

FHN64 uses the two-field reaction-diffusion system on $[0,32)$ with $D_u=1$, $D_v=0$, $\varepsilon=0.08$, $a=0.7$, and $b=0.8$. A periodic fourth-order centered stencil discretizes $\partial_{xx}$ on 64 points. Rodas5P uses `reltol=1e-10`, `abstol=1e-12`, and `dtmax=0.02`. The active, formation, collision, and recovery regimes have totals `240/96/96/48` and split-stratified counts.

Independent trajectories are integrated in parallel using deterministic per-system, per-trajectory seeds. HDF5 writes are serialized on the main thread. This preserves reproducibility across thread schedules while using the available 16 CPU threads.

## Validation Protocol

The formal gate checks finite values, exact tensor dimensions, trajectory-level split isolation, deterministic seed and initial-state uniqueness, fixed time grids, periodic symmetry, one-step production/reference agreement, early/late stationarity, autocorrelation time, effective sample size, and split-distribution consistency.

L96 and KS additionally use tangent propagation with periodic QR to estimate full Lyapunov spectra. Train-only PCA coordinates define the split-distribution space. Regularized Gaussian KLD and train-bandwidth RBF MMD compare validation and test distributions against train. KS and FHN compare one-step production states against `N_x=128` references.

## Formal-Run Corrections

The first formal run exposed a long-time KS implementation defect that the short smoke profile could not reveal. Without an explicit real-Hermitian projection after each ETDRK4 step, the nonphysical imaginary component grew from approximately `1e-15` to order one and eventually produced non-finite states. The state and tangent steps were corrected without changing `dt`, warm-up, domain, resolution, or any physical parameter. A 500-time-unit probe then kept the imaginary component near `1e-16`; the complete 480-trajectory KS regeneration passed.

The final common gate initially rejected all three valid time vectors. The original fixed tolerance `32eps(Float64)` was smaller than the `1.71e-14` subtraction residual accumulated at $t=256$. The gate was changed to the scale-aware machine-precision bound `8eps(maximum(abs, time))`. No trajectory or time value changed during certificate refresh.

## Interpretation And Limitations

The numerical time errors are well below the requested thresholds, and both chaotic systems have positive largest Lyapunov exponents. KS preserves its zero mode and has a negligible dealiased high-frequency tail. Split KLD and MMD values are small relative to the within-attractor scale, supporting common long-time distribution coverage.

The Lyapunov spectra use one deterministic long diagnostic trajectory per chaotic system rather than the suggested 16 L96 and 8 KS diagnostic-trajectory ensemble. The estimates are suitable as release health certificates but should not be treated as high-precision literature values.

The autonomous FHN configuration relaxes toward the resting neighborhood during long records. The active regime begins after a 32-unit warm-up with mean first-snapshot activity about `0.29`, but its full-record mean activity is low and the representative peak detector finds no positive pulses above its fixed threshold. Formation and collision trajectories provide the strongest transient propagation and interaction content. Downstream work requiring sustained wave trains should use the regime labels explicitly and may require a separately versioned forced or re-excited FHN protocol rather than changing this frozen raw release.

## Reproducibility

The release uses master seed `20260710`, deterministic system offsets and trajectory IDs, `Float64` throughout, and frozen `Project.toml`/`Manifest.toml` hashes in HDF5 metadata. The formal entry point is:

```powershell
julia --threads=16 --project=. experiments\data_generation\generate_highdim_nonlinear_v2_dataset.jl
```

Formal data are under `data/releases/highdim_nonlinear_v2/`; certificates are colocated with the HDF5 files, and human-readable evidence is under `reports/v2_core/highdim_nonlinear_v2/`.
